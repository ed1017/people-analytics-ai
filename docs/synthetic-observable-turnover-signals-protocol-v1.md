The question is whether an explicitly observable leading indicator helps a small fixed count model under a declared synthetic relationship, and when that benefit fails. A binary announced workload-pressure flag is drawn before outcome generation, measured May 31 and released June 3. A separate uniform draw chooses a later July–September extra-exit shock with probability .2 or .8 according to that flag. This is an imposed synthetic association, not a signal derived from future exits or an empirically established workload effect.

Train on seeds 4001–4100, January 2021–September 2025, with a June 30, 2025 forecast origin and labels available by October 31, 2025. Freeze the fitted models and training evidence in a commit before generating any test seed. Test on seeds 5001–5100, January 2021–September 2026, forecasting at June 30, 2026. Thus training labels precede the test forecast date and companies are disjoint. Ordinary reporting lags mean June counts are not model inputs.

Require at least ten scored training observations per cell; otherwise use the intercept correction. A missing or late signal also falls back to the intercept. Existing small-group suppression remains binding.

Generate informative and no-signal training scenarios and fit each separately. In the no-signal scenario the shock probability is .5 regardless of the flag. Scenarios reuse each seed's ordinary and shock continuations and assignment uniform, so they and the four company groups are not independent samples. Failure is retained, and success would be conditional synthetic evidence only. No significance, real-world accuracy, causal or uncertainty qualification claim follows.
