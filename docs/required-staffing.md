# Direct required-headcount comparisons

`compare_required_staffing` takes a stated role count directly. The existing workload-derived calculation is unchanged; a fixed requirement does not need invented contracts, ticket volume, productive hours or company population. Home retains typed scenario inputs and provenance through follow-ups and recomputes the numerical comparison in code. This is a provisional conversation comparison, not a saved Action Plan or an operational instruction.

Explicit count-and-strategy requests and numerical continuations of a current comparison require the staffing tool before a final response. Routing recognizes bounded English requirement phrases; it does not extract or invent the typed inputs. If the provider skips the required tool or the operation fails, the turn is rejected without changing prior state. Any model-selected staffing operation has the same completion requirement. Successful staffing calculations supply both the cards and the final numerical prose in code, preserving unknown training and readiness; unverified model wording cannot replace those results. Unrelated qualitative discussion remains available.

Hire cost is explicitly per person for the entered whole horizon. Training cash and hours are per trainee; incremental redeployment and internal backfill cash are per person. These are distinct from the older workload comparison's total-path training inputs. Period quotes are not annualized or prorated from unknown arrival dates. Currency changes require restated/cleared cash; horizon changes require restated/cleared period quotes; role changes require restated/cleared affected pools and rates. Missing fields stay null. Existing values change only through a current-user correction with retained provenance.

For up to 20 required people and 20 people per internal pool, the pure calculator enumerates at most 231 count-preserving combinations. Unknown internal pools are not enumerated and are labelled unknown. Known overlapping pools exclude combined internal routes; unverified overlap, release, readiness and role fit remain explicit conditions. The compact card shows at most three representative comparisons: all-hire, the lowest listed-cash eligible reference (or most-internal reference if no financial ranking is supported), and an intermediate training mix where available. Other combinations fill any remaining display slots. This is not a claim of operational optimization.

Known incremental cash is a subtotal. Unspecified backfill and other costs do not become zero or establish complete affordability. A known subtotal above the budget reports over budget; complete cash requires explicit cost coverage and all active cost components. A recommendation is conditional on the scenario rates and lists the next unresolved condition. Relative readiness offsets remain unknown unless supplied; an allocation of people does not establish productive coverage from period start.

Zero training included in an all-hire scenario does not establish zero training required. New-hire training hours remain “Not specified” unless explicitly supplied. Mixed scenarios show the planned trainee hours separately from unknown new-hire requirements. No productivity effect or skill readiness is inferred from training spend or hours.

The review-feedback regression declares illustrative inputs because the original live prompt was unavailable: ten required people, twelve months, USD 160,000 per hire for the period, USD 5,000 and 80 hours per trainee, zero incremental redeployment cash, separate pools of four redeployable and six trainable people. It produces listed cash of USD 1,600,000 for hire ten; USD 30,000 and 480 planned training hours for redeploy four/train six; USD 495,000 and 240 planned training hours for redeploy four/train three/hire three. Release, readiness, backfill, complete cost and new-hire training remain unknown in this fixture. These are scenario calculations, not database facts or reconstructed live-provider validation.

Offline tests cover the actual conversation service and POST for initial comparison, numerical follow-up and correction, plus direct arithmetic, provenance, stale scope, currency/horizon/role invalidation, React rendering and background desktop/phone interaction. Normal Home grounding still runs. This feature adds no data reader, salary feed, credential, grant, paid-provider test, model switch, retry or save/approval action. The review branch's automatic deployment is disabled.

An already selected standalone fixed-role comparison can finish after its first successful typed staffing calculation: the application constructs the existing deterministic final answer and runs the common final schema, scope and state validators. Saved-goal/plan contexts, existing proposals/analyses/constraints/metrics, mixed tool responses and opportunistic staffing calls retain the ordinary loop. Failed, oversized or missing calculations cannot use this completion path. While the staffing tool is forced, its schema alone is sent; grounding, full conversation context, instructions, model policy and output limit remain unchanged. This removes a redundant final generation; it is not a measured latency guarantee.
